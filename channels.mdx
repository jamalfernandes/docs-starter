## One job per surface

Seven surfaces are live. Each one gets a single job. A surface doing two jobs does neither, and that is exactly what the July and September audits found. Three properties described the same diagnostic work in three different vocabularies. The credibility sat on one domain and the commerce sat on another.

| Surface | Its one job | Who it is for | What it must never do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **xherman.com** | Sell the proof. Establish that Xavier's judgement is worth buying. | Someone who has just heard his name and is checking him before a conversation. | Carry price, mechanics, package lists, partner badges, or the AI Ops Engine. |
| **Experience Hacking** · xhacking.eu | Sell the method. The Blueprint, its five steps, the buyer's time cost, the price. | A reader already convinced by the person and now asking what the thing actually is. | Be the first surface a stranger meets. Go on advertising past-dated webinars as live, which it still does. |
| **LinkedIn** | Start the conversation the site finishes. | The owner or CEO inside his network and one degree out from it. | Speak in an agency voice. Replies in the thread are Xavier's, or they do not happen. |
| **Lex Index** | Nothing, here. One footer line that proves he ships products. | Lawyers, who do not arrive through anything in this kit. | Appear above the footer, or take a single slot in the weekly cadence. |
| **Friction Scanner** | Hold a qualified company that should not buy a Blueprint yet. | The SME with someone internally already driving AI, and the curious reader not ready to talk. | Be dressed up as a small Blueprint, or be described with the word *friction* to a reader who has not been through it. |
| **Writing** · the three published articles | Give a reader who wants more than a post somewhere to go next. | The reader who clicked twice and is still reading. | Run on a cadence Xavier cannot keep. Stay bylined to the team, which credits the brand instead of the man. |
| **Speaking** | Teach in the room. Sell nothing. | Event organisers, panel bookers, and the management team once a CEO opens the door. | Become a large-corporate pitch. That market is the one he is leaving. |

The AI Ops Engine is not a public surface anywhere in this kit. It is the delivery platform, and it appears once a project is already decided. `[xh-consulting.com and xhacking.app: retire, redirect, or keep, awaiting Xavier]`

## LinkedIn is the engine

Not because LinkedIn is a good channel in the abstract. Because three things Xavier already owns sit there and nowhere else.

His profile is the **first result on his name**, ahead of his own site, confirmed on 28 July. Whatever a prospect concludes about him, they conclude it there. His **fifteen recommendations** are there, the only third-party proof in the entire estate. And his **entry point has always been the CEO or business owner**. That is a network motion, and the network is on LinkedIn. Every deal so far came out of that network, and nothing in the record shows a channel outside it producing a single Blueprint enquiry.

Now the gap, honestly. We have never seen him post.

- The live profile was bot-blocked on 3 September (HTTP 999). We could not verify the headline, the About text or the recommendations. Everything in the record comes from a July PDF export, and LinkedIn exports omit recommendations entirely.
- The corpus holds no posting history at all: no frequency, no format, no reach. Any audience number in this kit would be invented, so there is none.
- His personal profile already runs Sales Navigator outreach automation for Lex Index, a venture explicitly out of scope. Nobody has resolved whether Blueprint content and that automation can share one profile. Until someone does, it constrains everything below.

## The weekly rhythm

Xavier consults full time and builds his own infrastructure. The cadence below is sized so that a busy fortnight does not break it. **Two posts a week, and thirty minutes of replies.** That is the whole commitment.

| When | What | How long |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every second Monday | Batch the next four posts from one real conversation each | 60 to 90 minutes |
| Tuesday and Thursday | Posts go out from the batch | Nothing, if batched |
| Across the week | Xavier replies to comments himself | 30 minutes, split how he likes |
| Monthly | Resurface one article that already exists. Write nothing new for it | 15 minutes |

The thirty minutes is the half nobody can do for him, and it is the half that produces conversations. A founder answering in the thread turns a reader into a conversation. An agency answering ends it. If that half hour is not there, the test is not running, and we say so at the time rather than reporting a failure six weeks later.

## Content mix

The 70/30 rule from the 4 September call governs every asset: roughly 70% the SME's current problems, 30% history and proof. It lives **inside each post**, not across the month. The mix below only decides which problem leads.

Across a month of eight posts:

| Share | Post type | What it opens with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ~35% | **The buyer's own sentence.** Something an owner actually said, and what it cost. | "We tried things with AI. Nothing worked." |
| ~25% | **Where AI does not pay off.** One idea that looks obvious and loses money. | The honest-sequencing line, in his flat register. |
| ~10% | **How to judge before spending.** The sizing arithmetic, two or three hours against twenty to thirty days. | A number, never an adjective. |
| ~20% | **Past to present.** One large-group programme, hinged into now. | The work, then the hinge. Never a CV. |
| ~10% | **Resurface.** One of the three published articles, or a speaking artefact. | The argument, not the announcement. |

The post skeleton, ready to fill. No call to action, no link on most posts.

## Organic is a test, not the strategy

This is the operating principle, and it is Xavier's framing. Organic is not dead. It is brutally competitive. So we run it as a free test to find what works before any money goes behind it, rather than as a channel we expect to carry the business on its own.

What that means in practice:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Publish to learn who answers" icon="users">
    **Who responds matters, not how many.** Fifty consultants liking a post is a failed test. Five SME owners replying is a passed one. Read every number as *of the right people*.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the gates down in advance" icon="ruler">
    We set the thresholds before the posts run, so nobody can grade the result generously afterwards. The August thresholds were written against a law-firm audience. They need restating against the SME owner before the next block starts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Spend only on what already worked" icon="coins">
    Paid launches on Xavier's date, with copy that has already proven itself organically. Buy traffic first and you pay to discover what a free post would have told you.
  </Step>
</Steps>

We commit to the cadence, the gates and the reporting. We do not promise lead volume from an organic test. Six weeks on one channel is a signal, not a forecast.
