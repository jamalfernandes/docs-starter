## The position in one paragraph

For the owner of a small or mid-sized company who suspects AI should be doing something and has no way to judge what, Xavier Herman is the consultant who says where the money comes back and where it does not. He spent thirty years running transformation programmes for Audi, Nike, Nestlé, Canal+ and Club Med. He now works directly with the people who run companies small enough to read in hours rather than weeks. The offer is the AI Blueprint: seven days, ending in a ranked roadmap that names the ideas not worth doing. Whoever carries the problem is the audience, whatever the job title says. Comex and Codir get the same motion.

## The brand in five lines

Every other page in this kit is written from these five rows. If a headline, a post or an email cannot be traced to one of them, it is off-brand.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Who it is for** | Whoever inside a small or mid-sized company carries the unanswered AI question. They believe AI should be doing something, cannot say what, cannot judge a quote, have already lost money on it or watched a peer do so. Not a job title. |
| **What he does** | Goes through the business with the people who run it and comes back with a ranked roadmap of where AI returns the money. Seven days. The offer is the AI Blueprint. |
| **Why it matters** | Reading a company of twenty-five or thirty people takes two to three hours. A large enterprise takes twenty to thirty days. Affordability and credibility come from the same fact, so we state the mechanism and never the word "affordable". |
| **How he sounds** | Direct, unhedged, euros not abstractions. He will tell you an idea is bad. He does not call AI a magic wand or claim certainty he lacks. |
| **What proves it** | The client names: Audi, Nike, Nestlé, Canal+, Club Med. Four LinkedIn recommendations pending sign-off: Calvet, Mars, Malige, Dewost. `[flagship AI-era reference, awaiting Xavier]` |

## The differentiator: honest sequencing

This is the block that decides whether the brand survives contact with a lawyer, a competitor or an unhappy buyer. Read it before writing anything.

<Warning>
**These phrases are dead. Do not publish them, do not paraphrase them, do not reintroduce them in a rewrite.**

- "no build to sell afterwards"
- "nothing to sell you afterwards"
- "no downstream interest"

Xavier corrected this on the 4 September call. There **is** a build to sell. The old line was false, and a false differentiator is worse than no differentiator.
</Warning>

The spine of the idea survives. Xavier tells you the truth before anyone asks you to buy anything. The difference is that the truth is now sequenced rather than refused. Here is the replacement line. It ships as written, in both languages.

<Tabs>

    ```text
    The Blueprint's job is to tell you where AI pays off, including when the
    answer is nowhere. What you do next is your call.
    ```
  </Tab>

  </Tab>

If a build is the right answer, Xavier says so and can provide it. That is why the sequencing is worth stating at all. The judgement comes first and carries its own price, so the buyer owns the decision before anyone quotes for the work. "No" is a legitimate output of the seven days.

## What the personal site refuses to say

The reader should finish xherman.com convinced Xavier is the right person and still unable to describe the method. That is the design. Say it out loud to anyone reviewing the page, or the omissions read as a gap.

| Not on the personal site | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Mechanics**: how the diagnostic runs, what tooling is involved | Spell out the method and the buyer starts comparing consultants on method. Experience Hacking already does that job properly. |
| **Price, packages, deliverable lists** | The offer is still moving. Xavier has sold deeper variants than the packaged one. Fixing it in public freezes it. |
| **Partner badges, HubSpot included** | Xavier is retiring the certified partnership. The wall carries clients only, never partners. |
| **AI Ops Engine** | The delivery platform, used once a project is already decided. Not a public-facing offer anywhere in this kit. |
| **Audience-size metrics**: followers, subscribers, readers | Creator-economy proof Xavier does not have and does not need. The client names are stronger. |
| **The word "friction" in SME-facing lines** | It only lands with readers mature enough to have been through it. |

## The two sites

One argument, split across two domains. Personal site sells the proof. Experience Hacking sells the method.

| | xherman.com | Experience Hacking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sells** | The person. Why Xavier's judgement is worth buying. | The method. What the Blueprint is and how it runs. |
| **Carries** | Track record, client names, recommendations, the honest-sequencing line | Mechanics, deliverables, the price |
| **Content mix** | ~70% the SME's current problems, ~30% history and proof | Method detail for a reader already further along |
| **Ends in** | A conversation | A transaction |

That split is the whole reason for running two domains. It lets the personal site carry thirty years of enterprise credibility without the reader concluding Xavier is out of reach. The number that would trigger that conclusion sits one click away, in the hero of a different site.
