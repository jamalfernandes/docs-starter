## The position in one paragraph

For the owner of a small or mid-sized company who suspects AI should be doing something and has no way to judge what, Xavier Herman is the consultant who says where the money comes back and where it does not. He built digital and AI transformation on the agency side, for Audi, Nike, Nestlé, Nissan, Canal+ and Club Med, then ran it from the inside as head of digital at Caudalie, Derichebourg and Colibee. He now works directly with people running companies small enough to read in hours rather than weeks. The offer is the AI Blueprint, a custom diagnostic ending in a ranked roadmap that names the ideas not worth doing. Whoever carries the problem is the audience, whatever the job title says.

<Warning icon="users">
  **Two different relationships, and they must never be blurred.** Xavier's 7 October correction, and it is the easiest thing on this page to get wrong.

  | Companies | The relationship | The wording |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Audi, Nike, Nestlé, Nissan, Canal+, Club Med | Clients of the agencies he led. A2X for Audi and Nike; Kick Your App for the rest | **"on the agency side, for"**. Never "inside", never "at" |
  | Caudalie, Derichebourg Multiservices, Colibee | Employed roles. He ran the function | **"from the inside, as head of digital"** |

  "Head of digital" is a deliberate simplification Xavier has endorsed. The real titles are Global Head of Digital at Caudalie, Chief Digital Officer at Derichebourg Multiservices, and Directeur Marketing et Digital at Colibee. Recorded here so nobody over-claims upward from the simplification.

  The phrase **"interim director"** is retired. It is in no current source and it reads as a stand-in rather than a role.
</Warning>

## The brand in five lines

Every other page is written from these five rows. If a headline, post or email cannot be traced to one of them, it is off-brand.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Who it is for | Whoever inside a small or mid-sized company carries the unanswered AI question. They believe AI should be doing something, cannot say what, cannot judge a quote, and have already lost money on it or watched a peer do so. Not a job title |
| What he does | Goes through the business with the people who run it and comes back with a ranked roadmap of where AI returns the money. The offer is the AI Blueprint |
| Why it matters | Reading a company of up to 50 people takes two to three hours. A large enterprise takes twenty to thirty days. Affordability and credibility come from the same fact, so state the mechanism and never the word "affordable" |
| How he sounds | Direct, unhedged, euros not abstractions. He will tell you an idea is bad. He does not call AI a magic wand or claim certainty he lacks |
| What proves it | Three engagements published with their fees: Fred & Fly €990, Smart Shopper €4,200, SLS Agency €5,400. SLS runs in full, with 600 points of sale, RFID killed and computer vision chosen. Plus the ten recommendations carried on his live site |

## The differentiator: honest sequencing

Read this before writing anything. It decides whether the brand survives contact with a lawyer, a competitor or an unhappy buyer.

<Warning icon="ban">
  **These phrases are dead.** Do not publish them, paraphrase them, or reintroduce them in a rewrite.

  - "no build to sell afterwards"
  - "nothing to sell you afterwards"
  - "no downstream interest"

  Xavier corrected this on 4 September. There **is** a build, and it is the AI Ops Engine. A false differentiator is worse than no differentiator.
</Warning>

The spine survives. Xavier tells you the truth before anyone asks you to buy anything. What changed is that the truth is sequenced rather than refused. The replacement ships as written, in both languages.

## What the personal site refuses to say

The reader should finish xherman.com convinced Xavier is the right person and still unable to describe the method. That is the design. Say it out loud to anyone reviewing the page, or the omissions read as a gap.

| Not on the personal site | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Mechanics: how the diagnostic runs, what tooling is involved | Spell out the method and the buyer starts comparing consultants on method. Experience Hacking already does that job properly |
| Packages and fixed deliverable lists | The offer is custom. A discovery call sets the scope, then the fee. Fixing a package in public freezes it, which is what the €990 product did before it was retired |
| Partner badges, HubSpot included | Xavier is retiring the certified partnership. The wall carries clients only |
| AI Ops Engine | The delivery platform, used once a project is decided. Not public-facing anywhere in this kit |
| Audience-size metrics: followers, subscribers, readers | Creator-economy proof Xavier does not have and does not need. The client names are stronger |
| The word "friction" in SME-facing lines | It only lands with readers mature enough to have been through it |

<Note icon="euro">
  **Price is now on the personal site**, which reverses an earlier rule. From €990 excluding VAT, always paired with a real engagement so nobody anchors on the floor. The figure was always one click away on Experience Hacking, and hiding it while publishing it elsewhere read as evasive.
</Note>

## The two sites

One argument split across two domains. The personal site sells the proof. Experience Hacking sells the method.

|  | xherman.com | Experience Hacking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sells | The person. Why his judgement is worth buying | The method. What the Blueprint is and how it runs |
| Carries | Track record, client names, recommendations, the entry price, the honest-sequencing line | Mechanics, the delivery spec, deliverables |
| Content mix | ~70% the SME's current problems, ~30% history and proof | Method detail for a reader already further along |
| Ends in | A conversation | A transaction |

That split is the whole reason for running two domains. It lets the personal site carry enterprise credibility without the reader concluding Xavier is out of reach.
