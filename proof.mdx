## How this page works

Every claim the kit makes sits in the register below with a status. Three statuses, and only three.

<Columns cols={3}>
  <Card title="PUBLISHED" icon="globe">
    Already live on a public surface today. Xavier has put his name to it, so repeating it carries no new risk. Copy it as it stands.
  </Card>

  <Card title="HELD" icon="file-text">
    We hold a verbatim source in the client folder: a transcript line, a live capture, a published page. Safe to state, and safe to quote if you reproduce the words exactly.
  </Card>

  <Card title="AWAITING" icon="circle-dashed">
    No source we can quote from. It cannot ship in any form. The slot stays visibly empty until Xavier sends the source.
  </Card>
</Columns>

**The rule under all of it:** nothing goes inside quotation marks under a named person unless those exact words appear in a source file. Not a summary. Not an English gloss of a French original. Not an analyst's description converted into speech. The words, or nothing.

An empty proof slot is a fine thing to ship. An invented one ends the engagement.

## The AI-era gap, closed

This was the most important finding on the page. It is now the most important thing that changed.

**The gap, as it stood.** None of the fifteen LinkedIn recommendations mentioned AI. The newest was October 2021. Seven of the fifteen were written inside a ten-week window in early 2008. Every one corroborated the previous narrative: digital transformation, marketing management, B2B mobile. The proof set validated the Xavier he was moving away from.

It blocked launch, because the page argues for judgement about AI and had no voice other than Xavier's saying so.

## Two fabrications caught, and what changed

Twice in this project, words that exist in no source were attributed to a real, named, living person.

**The first.** An early mockup carried a line attributed to a former colleague of Xavier's. It was invented. It was caught at the 12 August deck stage and removed, and the standing instruction since then was that no words could be attributed to him at all. On 7 October he came out of the kit entirely: he was never a client or a business partner, so there was no claim left to protect.

**The second.** Draft one of the AI Blueprint site copy ran a four-clause English quotation under Jérôme Calvet's name. Two of those clauses appear in no source in this folder or anywhere else. They came from an analyst's descriptive prose in the 10 August ICP document, a sentence about how the recommendation "moves through the diagnostic as revelation". That sentence hardened into quoted client speech across the 12 August deck and expanded again in the 3 September mockup. It was caught on 3 September and removed.

Neither reached a published page. Both would have put invented words in quotation marks under a real former client's name on a live site.

**What changed as a result.** Quotation marks now require a verbatim source, full stop. Glosses and translations are labelled as Boost33's work wherever they appear. Proof slots that have no source are left visibly empty and marked, rather than filled with something plausible. This register exists so that the status of every claim is written down somewhere a person can check, instead of living in whoever last edited the file.

If Xavier asks why the proof band sat empty for a month, the answer is this, given straight:

> An earlier draft had a testimonial in quotation marks under Jérôme Calvet's name, and two of those sentences do not exist in any source. We found it and removed it. Nothing went on the page in quotes unless it was in a recording or already published, which is why those blocks stayed empty rather than getting filled. They are full now, and every word in them came off your profile.

**The band filled on 21 September**, when all fifteen recommendations came back verbatim and source-tagged. That is the right order: the rule held, then the evidence arrived, and nothing plausible was invented in between.
