## The two sites

The most common mistake will be writing the wrong thing for the wrong property. One decision on 4 September settles it, and it governs every page, post and email here.

|  | Personal site · xherman.com | Experience Hacking · xhacking.eu |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sells | The proof. Why Xavier | The method. What the Blueprint is |
| Carries | History, track record, references, the sizing argument, the entry price | Mechanics, the delivery spec, deliverables |
| Never carries | Technical mechanics, partner badges, the AI Ops Engine | No equivalent restriction |
| Reader leaves with | Conviction that he is the right person | Understanding of what they are buying |

## The one rule above all others

**Specifics instead of adjectives.** Every claim carries a number, a name or a source.

This is not a style preference. Xavier's audience is business owners who check. His own instinct is to correct a claim the moment it overstates, and he did that on the 4 September call against his own interest. Copy that overstates does not sound like him and will not survive contact with him.

<Warning icon="shield-alert">
  Nothing goes on a page in quotation marks unless it exists verbatim in a recording or on a published page. An earlier draft ran a testimonial under a real client's name containing sentences that were in no source. It was caught and removed. That standard is why the proof blocks sat empty for a month, and why they are now full of text that came straight off his profile.
</Warning>

## How to use it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the block you need" icon="search">
    Use the search bar. Every ready-to-use text sits in a copy block, so nothing gets retyped.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill the brackets" icon="code">
    Anything in `[square brackets]` needs a real detail from Xavier. A post with an invented number does more damage than no post, because this audience checks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check it against the voice rules" icon="list-checks">
    One pass over [the rules that do not bend](/voice). If a draft cannot be traced to a [pillar](/messaging), and that pillar to a source, it does not publish.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build from the token sheet" icon="ruler">
    The [style sheet](/visual) is copy-ready CSS. It drops straight into the repo, so nothing gets interpreted between this kit and the build.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What changed on 21 September

Xavier returned seven pages of written corrections. Four were factual, and one was a live credibility risk.

| Was | Now |
| --- | --- |
| "First inside large groups at Audi, Nike, Nestlé, Canal+, Club Med" | Built it for them from the agency side, then ran it from the inside elsewhere |
| Companies of "two to ten" people | Independents and companies up to twenty to fifty, depending on sector |
| "One week" | Two to four weeks. Three is typical |
| "I've got no build to sell you after" | False. The AI Ops Engine is exactly that build |
| "It ends in a decision, not a document" | It ends in a Project Reference Document |

<Check icon="circle-check">
  The same day unblocked the proof stack. All fifteen LinkedIn recommendations came back verbatim and source-tagged, and Eric Bécart's September 2026 reference names the AI Blueprint directly. That closed the oldest item on this project.
</Check>

## Language

Xavier publishes in English and French. This kit is written in English for the Boost33 team, and every ready-to-use block carries an FR tab beside the EN original.

<Note icon="languages">
  The French is written, not translated. Short sentences stay short, _vous_ throughout, and no anglicism Xavier would not say out loud. He knows how his own market talks, so he reads the FR before anything publishes.
</Note>
