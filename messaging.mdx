## The core message

> **Xavier Herman tells the owner of a small company where AI will pay off and where it will not, before a single euro goes into a build.**

Two parts of that sentence do the work.

_Where it will not_ is the half almost nobody else says out loud. Most consultants sell the build, so they cannot afford to tell you the build is a bad idea.

_Before a single euro_ sets the order. Diagnosis first. The decision stays with the buyer, and if a build is the right answer Xavier says so and can do it.

<Info icon="circle-alert">
  Earlier drafts said Xavier has "no build to sell afterwards." He corrected that on 4 September: there is a build, and it is the AI Ops Engine. Never write "no build to sell", "nothing to sell you afterwards", or "no downstream interest" on any surface.
</Info>

## The four pillars

Every asset argues one of these. If a draft maps to none of them, cut it.

<Accordion title="1. Where the money comes back, and where it does not" icon="coins">
  **Claim.** The Blueprint ranks where AI returns money in this business, and names the ideas that will not work.

  **Answers.** The owner who has already spent on an AI tool nobody uses.

  **Proof.** SLS Agency arrived certain they needed RFID tags on 600 display stands. Fully costed, sound logic. Xavier killed it, because the real problem was that nobody in store had time to count, not that nothing was being tracked. Their CEO names this as the thing he valued: _"his ability to challenge our initial ideas, to separate immediately actionable solutions from more ambitious projects."_

  **Status: published.** SLS business case, 10 July 2026, plus Eric Bécart's recommendation, 18 September 2026.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="2. Affordable because your company is small enough to read" icon="ruler">
  **Claim.** Auditing a large group takes 20 to 30 days before anything starts. A company of up to 50 people takes two or three hours to understand.

  **Answers.** The reader thinking, in Xavier's own report of it, _"you work for all these big groups, I'm a small company, the guy is unaffordable."_

  **Proof.** Xavier, 12 August, verbatim, with both halves in one sentence. The price now backs it up in public, across three published engagements: €990 for Fred & Fly at one week, €4,200 for Smart Shopper and €5,400 for SLS Agency, both at three weeks.

  **Status: held.** One thing still open. Whether the €20K enterprise audit figure goes on a public page is Xavier's call: `[enterprise cost anchor, awaiting Xavier]`.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="3. Enterprise depth, now working at your size" icon="building-2">
  **Claim.** Xavier built this judgement around large groups. He now points it at companies small enough to understand in an afternoon.

  **Answers.** Why this person rather than the local agency.

  **Proof.** The client wall, live on xherman.com, under Xavier's own published caption: _"They trusted me throughout my entrepreneurial, consulting, and transition journeys."_

  **Status: published.** The names are published and real, and the years figure resolved on 7 October: **30 years** for Xavier, **+15 years** for XH Consulting. Each belongs to its entity and the two are never swapped.

Caudalie, Derichebourg and Colibee were **inside roles, as head of digital**. See the table on [the brand platform](/positioning).

<Accordion title="4. It ends in a decision you can act on" icon="file-check">
  **Claim.** You leave with a Project Reference Document. Comparative scenarios, CAPEX and OPEX on each, a clear go or no-go per option tested, and a roadmap.

  **Answers.** Two fears at once. An open-ended retainer, and a fifty-slide deck nobody reads.

  **Proof.** Those four components are the documented SLS deliverable, named in the published business case. That engagement ran three weeks and five meetings, as a documented example rather than a typical one. Bécart's line is the outcome: _"a solid basis for deciding our next investments."_

  **Status: published.** This pillar used to read "Seven days, and you keep what you find," proved by nothing more than the old page repeating the number.

The second half turned out to be right: **"the findings are yours either way" is approved**, because it is published verbatim on his own home page in both languages. It was blocked here for a month as a borrowed device. It was his.
</Accordion>

<Tip icon="scale">
  Pillar 4 is the one that sells. The Blueprint hands a client a scoped cost and timeline, which is exactly what they need to get budget approved internally for the build. It is a diagnostic that pays for the next decision.
</Tip>

## The 70/30 rule

Decided on the 4 September call. Collateral runs roughly 70% the reader's current problems, 30% Xavier's record. History earns the right to be heard. It is not the argument. The split applies per asset, not per campaign.

|  | The 70 | The 30 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject | The reader's business today | Xavier's record, past tense |
| Voice | The buyer's own words | An explicit "now" hinge |
| Looks like | "Money has already gone into an AI tool nobody uses." | "Programmes run inside large groups. Now working at the size of your company." |
| Fails when | It turns into generic AI category education | It turns into a CV, a year count, or a logo wall above the fold |
