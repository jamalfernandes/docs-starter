## The core message

> **Xavier Herman tells the owner of a small company where AI will pay off and where it will not, before a single euro goes into a build.**

**Where it will not** is the half almost nobody else says out loud. The Blueprint sells judgement, not construction.

**Before a single euro** sets the order. The diagnosis comes first. The decision is the buyer's, and if a build is the right answer Xavier says so and can provide it.

<Info>
  **This replaces the old line.** Earlier drafts said Xavier has "no build to sell afterwards."
  Xavier corrected that on 4 September: there is a build. Never write "no build to sell",
  "nothing to sell you afterwards", or "no downstream interest" on any surface.
</Info>

## The four pillars

Every asset argues one of these. If a draft maps to none of them, cut it.

### 1. Where the money comes back, and where it does not

**Claim.** The Blueprint ranks where AI returns money in this business, and names the ideas that will not work.

**Answers.** The owner who has already spent on an AI tool nobody uses.

**Proof.** Xavier, 12 August, verbatim: *"out of the blue, your idea is a very bad idea. Just don't do that. You're just going to waste your money."*

**Status: HELD.** Verbatim in the source bank. Usable as reported speech; a cleaned version needs sign-off as a rewrite.

### 2. Affordable because your company is small enough to read

**Claim.** A large group takes 20 to 30 days to audit before anything starts, roughly a €20K budget. A company of 25 to 30 people takes two or three hours to understand.

**Answers.** The reader thinking, in Xavier's report of it, *"you work for all these big groups, I'm a small company, the guy is unaffordable."*

**Proof.** Xavier, 12 August, verbatim, with both numbers in one sentence.

**Status: HELD, PUBLICATION AWAITING XAVIER.** Whether the enterprise cost anchor appears publicly is his call: `[cost anchor, publish or not, awaiting Xavier]`.

### 3. Enterprise depth, now working at your size

**Claim.** Xavier built this judgement inside large groups. He now points it at companies small enough to understand in an afternoon.

**Answers.** Why this person rather than the local agency.

**Proof.** The client wall, live on xherman.com, under Xavier's own published caption: *"They trusted me throughout my entrepreneurial, consulting, and transition journeys."*

**Status: PARTLY HELD.** Names are published and real. The numbers are not settled: `[experience figure, awaiting Xavier]`, `[client project count, awaiting Xavier]`. Write every line so removing the number changes nothing.

### 4. Seven days, and you keep what you find

**Claim.** The first engagement is seven days. Flat. You leave with a ranked plan and a list of what to drop.

**Answers.** The fear of an open-ended retainer and a fifty-slide deck.

**Proof.** The Blueprint page publishes seven days in both languages.

**Status: DURATION HELD. "FINDINGS ARE YOURS" UNSOURCED.** That promise is a borrowed device. Xavier has not said it in any source we hold: `[findings-are-yours commitment, awaiting Xavier]`.

## The 70/30 rule

Decided on the 4 September call. All collateral runs roughly **70% the SME's current problems, 30% Xavier's history and proof.** History earns the right to be heard. It is not the argument. The split applies per asset, not per campaign.

| | The 70 | The 30 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Subject** | The reader's business today | Xavier's record, past tense |
| **Voice** | The buyer's own words | Explicit "now" hinge |
| **Looks like** | "Money has already gone into an AI tool nobody uses." | "Programmes run inside large groups. Now working at the size of your company." |
| **Fails when** | It becomes generic AI category education | It becomes a CV, a year count, or a logo wall above the fold |
