Three sequences, ten emails, written to send as they are once the greeting and sign-off come back from Xavier. Every code block is paste-ready copy in both languages. The prose around the blocks is the working reference for the Boost33 team.

## Rules that apply to all three

1. **One ask per email.** If a reader has to choose between two actions, most choose neither.
2. **No price in the body.** Price lives on Experience Hacking. Emails link to the page and stop there.
3. **Seven days, flat.** No _typically_, no _environ_, in any email, in either language.
4. **Never "no build to sell".** There is a build. What sets Xavier apart is the order he works in. He says where AI pays off first, including when the answer is nowhere, and the reader decides what happens next.
5. **"Friction Scanner" is a product name. "Friction" is not a word.** Use the proper noun for the tool and never the common noun in body copy. His own note, 12 August: the term only lands with buyers who have already been through it.
6. **70/30.** Roughly seven lines about the reader's business for every three about Xavier's record.

## Sequence 1 · Friction Scanner follow-up

**Trigger.** The Friction Scanner report has been delivered. The scanner sends that report within five minutes, so the sequence starts after it, not with it.

**Goal.** Stay useful and earn the next conversation. This reader is not ready for a Blueprint, and pushing one now turns them into an unsubscribe. Three of the four emails ask for nothing but a reply.

**Timing.**

| Email | Sends | Ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Day 2 | Reply with what the report said |
| 2 | Day 6 | None |
| 3 | Day 13 | None |
| 4 | Day 25 | Reply, or nothing |

**Branching.**

- **Any reply** pauses the sequence immediately and routes to Xavier as a human conversation. Do not let an automation answer a person who wrote back.
- **Two or more visits to the Blueprint page** exits to Sequence 2 and skips whatever is left here.
- **No opens across emails 1 to 3** still receives email 4, then moves to the low-frequency list. No fifth email.
- **Unsubscribe** removes from all three sequences, not just this one.

## Sequence 2 · Blueprint enquiry to booked call

**Trigger.** Someone asks about the AI Blueprint: the contact form, a reply to any email, or a promotion out of Sequence 1.

**Goal.** Get the call booked. The call is twenty minutes and its stated job is to check the Blueprint is the right thing, which is also what makes it easy to accept.

**Timing.**

| Email | Sends | Ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Within one business day of the enquiry | Book |
| 2 | Day 3 | Book |
| 3 | Day 7 | Book, or say "not this quarter" |

**Branching.**

- **Call booked** exits immediately. The confirmation and reminder are the booking tool's job, not this sequence's.
- **Reply with a question** pauses everything and routes to Xavier. Email 3 exists partly to invite exactly this.
- **"Not this quarter"** stops all sending and sets a single reminder for the following quarter. Nothing automated in between. The email promises this, so the system has to honour it.
- **No action after email 3** moves to the low-frequency list. There is no fourth email and no re-entry unless they enquire again.

**The affordability handling.** Price is named nowhere in the body. Email 1 answers the fear with commitment size, because a reader who fears an open-ended retainer is answered by a duration, not a number. Email 2 links to the page for the figure, then argues the objection underneath it. That objection is rarely the price. It is whether a person with this record is sized for this buyer.

## Sequence 3 · Post-Blueprint follow-through

**Trigger.** The activation plan session is done and the roadmap is delivered. Day 7 of the engagement is day 0 of this sequence.

**Goal.** Two goals, in this order. First, the roadmap gets used, because an unused plan is worth nothing to the buyer and produces no reference for Xavier. Second, the door to a build stays open honestly. Xavier names what the buyer's own team should do, what is worth building, and when the answer is neither.

**Timing.**

| Email | Sends | Ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Day 3 after delivery | A name and a date |
| 2 | Day 28 | One line on what happened |
| 3 | Day 60 | Which of three, in a reply |

**Branching.**

- **Reply to email 1 with a name and a date** keeps them in the sequence. Email 2 still sends, because the three-week check is where plans stall.
- **No reply to email 1** also keeps them in. Email 2 is written to work either way, which is why it has two openings inside one email rather than two separate versions.
- **They ask about building at any point** exits the sequence to a scoping conversation with Xavier. The automation stops and a person takes over.
- **They say the roadmap was wrong** exits to Xavier directly and is logged as a delivery issue, not a marketing one. Email 2 names this as one of three possible answers on purpose, so it gets said rather than swallowed.

## What to measure

One primary measure per sequence. Everything else is diagnostic and exists to tell you which email to rewrite.

| Sequence | Primary measure | Secondary | Diagnostic | Rewrite when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · Scanner follow-up** | Replies per hundred entering the sequence | Visits to the Blueprint page, and promotions into Sequence 2 | Unsubscribes attributed to a single email | One email carries most of the unsubscribes, or email 4 produces the only replies |
| **2 · Enquiry to call** | Calls booked per hundred entering the sequence | "Not this quarter" replies, counted as a wanted outcome rather than a loss | Which email the booking followed | Email 2 books nobody. It is the affordability argument under test, and a null result there is the finding |
| **3 · Post-Blueprint** | Roadmap items started by day 28, self-reported in replies | Scoping conversations opened out of email 3 | Which of the three stop reasons in email 2 comes back most | The same stop reason repeats across buyers. That is a delivery problem in the Blueprint, not an email problem |
