## The personality

Xavier is precise and unhurried. He explains something by walking through how it works, in order, with the numbers in: twenty to thirty days to audit a large group, two or three hours to read a company of thirty people. He does not push and he does not overstate. When something is wrong he corrects it on the spot, in the same flat tone he uses for everything else.

On the 4 September call he did it against his own interest. Told that his differentiator was having no build to sell afterwards, he answered **"And to say, I have no build to sell afterwards, I do, actually"**, then **"I understand the point of view, it's just that it's not exactly correct."** _(Xavier, verbatim, 4 September transcript `@2:34` and `@2:54`.)_ He gave up a strong marketing line because it was not true. That instinct is the voice. He turns it on himself too. On whether the Blueprint will scale, his own words are **"I do not know yet, I have no proof yet"** _(verbatim, 12 August `@17:38`)_.

## Six rewrites

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="EN" icon="pen-line">
    **1. Hedged duration**

    ~~The Blueprint typically takes around a week, depending on scope.~~

    > The Blueprint is seven days.

    *Three hedges in one sentence tell the reader the number is negotiable. It is not.*

    ---

    **2. The refusal that was not true**

    ~~Unlike every other AI consultant, I have no build to sell you afterwards.~~

    > My job is to tell you where AI pays off, including when the answer is nowhere. What you do next is your call.

    *Xavier corrected the first version himself on 4 September. The spine survives, the false claim goes.*

    ---

    **3. Adjective instead of arithmetic**

    ~~My approach is uniquely affordable for smaller businesses.~~

    > A large group takes twenty to thirty days just to audit. A company of thirty people takes me two or three hours. That is the whole reason this is affordable.

    *"Affordable" is a claim. The arithmetic is the proof, and it is his.*

    ---

    **4. Claiming more certainty than he has**

    ~~A proven method, scaling across hundreds of SMEs.~~

    > Short engagements at a low entry price. Whether that scales, I will know when I have run more of them.

    *His own words on this are "I do not know yet, I have no proof yet" (verbatim, 12 Aug `@17:38`). Copy may not be more confident than he is.*
  </Tab>

    **5. L'accroche gonflée**

    ~~Grâce à une approche innovante et sur mesure, libérez tout le potentiel de l'IA dans votre entreprise.~~

    > En sept jours, vous saurez où l'IA sera rentable chez vous. Et où elle ne le sera pas.

## Rules that do not bend

1. **Specifics instead of adjectives.** Every claim carries a number, a name or a source. If you cannot attach one, cut the sentence.
2. **Seven days, stated flat.** No *typically*, no *around*, no *depending on scope*, no *environ*. Every language, every surface.
3. **Never claim there is nothing to sell afterwards.** There is a build and Xavier can provide it. _No build to sell_ and _no downstream interest_ are banned, along with every variant of either. The differentiator is honest sequencing, not refusal.
4. **No price on the personal site.** Price lives on Experience Hacking. The personal site may state duration and the buyer's time cost, because those are commitment rather than mechanism.
5. **No invented proof.** Nothing goes in quotation marks unless it exists verbatim in a recording or on a live page. An empty slot marked `[awaiting Xavier]` is acceptable. A filled one that is false is not.
6. **Define the reader by the problem, not the title.** His own instruction: SME roles vary too much between companies for personas to hold.
7. **Do not use "friction" with an SME reader.** His own observation, 12 August `@34:25`. It only lands with buyers who have been through it.
8. **Never clean his phrasing silently.** Tidying a transcript line for print is a rewrite, needs his sign-off, and may not be presented as verbatim.
