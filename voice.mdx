## The personality

Xavier is precise and unhurried. He explains something by walking through how it works, in order, with the numbers in. Twenty to thirty days to audit a large group. Two or three hours to read a company of thirty people. He does not push and he does not overstate. When something is wrong he corrects it on the spot, in the same flat tone he uses for everything else.

On the 4 September call he did it against his own interest. Told that his differentiator was having no build to sell afterwards, he answered **"And to say, I have no build to sell afterwards, I do, actually"**, then **"I understand the point of view, it's just that it's not exactly correct."** _(Verbatim, 4 September transcript `@2:34` and `@2:54`.)_

He gave up a strong marketing line because it was not true. That instinct is the voice, and he turns it on himself too. On whether the Blueprint will scale, his own words are **"I do not know yet, I have no proof yet"** _(12 August `@17:38`)_.

## Registers by surface

The personality never changes. The amount of mechanism does.

| Surface | Its job | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal site | Sell the proof. Why Xavier. | First person. The longest sentences on any surface. History is allowed in. No mechanics. |
| Experience Hacking | Sell the method. | Second person and imperative. Stages, deliverables, the delivery spec. Product voice, not personal voice. |
| LinkedIn | Start the conversation the site finishes. | The shortest register. One observation from a real conversation, one line of consequence. No CTA on most posts. |
| Email | Move one person one step. | Named and direct. Says what he wants in the first three lines. |
| Speaking | Teach in the room, sell nothing. | Slowest. Examples before conclusions. Questions back to the room. |

What never changes: specifics instead of adjectives, and no claim he cannot show.

## Words that matter

Not a style sheet. These are the ones this project keeps getting wrong.

### Use

| EN | FR | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| where AI pays off, and where it does not | où l'IA est rentable, et où elle ne l'est pas | The whole offer in one clause |
| a euro | un euro | His unit. He reasons in euros, never in "budget" |
| AI is not a magic wand | l'IA n'est pas une baguette magique | Verbatim Xavier, 12 Aug. Lands harder in French |
| you hand it the keys | vous lui donnez les clés | The image of the failure mode |
| before you spend | avant de dépenser | Puts the reader ahead of the mistake |
| a ranked plan | un plan priorisé | A deliverable, not an outcome |
| what you do next is your call | la suite vous appartient | The honest-sequencing line |

### Do not use

| Never | Instead |
| --- | --- |
| nothing to sell you afterwards | Banned outright. There is a build |
| seven days, or any fixed duration | One to four weeks, depending on format |
| "two to four weeks", "three is typical" | Superseded 7 October. One to four weeks |
| independents, as the Blueprint's audience | Companies of up to 50 people |
| interim director | Head of digital, and only for Caudalie, Derichebourg and Colibee |
| friction, to an SME reader | His own note: it only lands with buyers who have been through it |
| unlock, leverage, harness, empower | Name the place the money comes back |
| revolutionary, game-changer, cutting-edge | A number, or a date |
| seamless, frictionless, robust, holistic | Describe the step that disappears |
| scalable | He has no proof yet. His words, not ours |
| our solutions | One offer. Name it |
| in today's fast-paced landscape | Delete. Start at the next sentence |

Anything not on these lists gets judged by the same test: does it carry a number, a name or a source? If not, cut it.

## Rules that do not bend

1. **Specifics instead of adjectives.** Every claim carries a number, a name or a source. If you cannot attach one, cut the sentence.
2. **Never claim there is nothing to sell afterwards.** There is a build and Xavier can provide it. The differentiator is honest sequencing, not refusal.
3. **Never state a fixed duration.** Engagements run **one to four weeks** depending on format, with or without an experimentation track. That is the wording on his live site, in both languages. The SLS engagement, three weeks and five meetings, is a **documented example** and may never be called typical. One case is not a distribution.
4. **Price appears as a floor, never alone.** From €990 excluding VAT, always next to a real engagement. On its own it anchors the reader at the bottom and makes the true number feel like an upsell.
5. **No invented proof.** Nothing goes in quotation marks unless it exists verbatim in a recording or on a live page. An empty slot marked `[awaiting Xavier]` is fine. A filled one that is false is not.
6. **Define the reader by the problem, not the title.** His own instruction. SME roles vary too much between companies for personas to hold.
7. **Never clean his phrasing silently.** Tidying a transcript line for print is a rewrite. It needs his sign-off and may not be presented as verbatim.
